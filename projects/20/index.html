Unobravo Redesign

Unobravo connects people with therapists over video call. Its website tried to be a marketing page, a guide and an onboarding flow at once, and the app offered no explanation of the service at all. I redesigned both, from interviews to a tested Figma prototype.

01

Context

Unobravo is a leading European online psychology service, founded in Italy in 2019, that matches people with licensed therapists over video call. Many potential users hesitate: they worry about privacy, doubt that online therapy can feel human, and don't understand how the matching works. The goal was to reduce that reluctance and make the service easy to understand and trust, across ages and levels of digital literacy.

03

Persona

Young adult (25–34) · Preparation

Luca Taglioni

29 · graphic designer in Milan · high digital proficiency · anxiety, panic attacks, sleep difficulties

“There is hope, even when your brain tells you there isn't.”

Friends in therapy have encouraged Luca to start, and he feels ready. He expects polished, personal digital products and dislikes rigid, bureaucratic flows. He will invest time and money, but only if the platform feels trustworthy and human.

  • →A clean, professional interface that still feels warm, not clinical
  • →Filters and recommendations tailored to anxiety and sleep issues
  • →A frictionless booking flow with availability, cost and policies up front
  • →Reassuring, empathetic microcopy for a first-time therapy user
04

Findings

Assessment, expert review and preliminary testing surfaced nine usability issues, each scored for impact and persistence (1–5).

  1. I01
    Overwhelming and unclear “How it works” experienceNo tested user could explain how Unobravo works. “Oh my god, there is so much stuff here.”
    ImpactPersistence
  2. I02
    Calls to action push people into the questionnaire too earlyUsers clicked “Trova il tuo Unobravo” expecting an explanation, and landed in the test.
    ImpactPersistence
  3. I03
    Matching logic is opaqueThe promised three profiles were not delivered, and users were forced to retake the test.
    ImpactPersistence
  4. I04
    Scheduling ignores stated preferencesA morning preference was ignored, with no explanation when slots were unavailable.
    ImpactPersistence
  5. I05
    Changing therapist means retaking the whole testUsers repeated the full questionnaire just to change availability.
    ImpactPersistence
  6. I06
    No way to compare therapistsThe “compare profiles” task failed for every user; the index was missing from the app.
    ImpactPersistence
  7. I07
    Pricing and privacy are hard to findUsers searched the wrong sections and misremembered prices; privacy lived only in the footer.
    ImpactPersistence
  8. I08
    Weak navigation and no escape routesBack options and orientation cues were missing along key flows.
    ImpactPersistence
  9. I09
    App navigation hidden in a sidebarThe app offered no explanation of the service and no dashboard for ongoing therapy.
    ImpactPersistence
05

Design principles

R01 · addresses I01, I02

Separate explaining from converting

A non-transactional “Scopri Unobravo” path explains the service, matching, costs and privacy before anyone is asked to start the test.

R02 · addresses I02

Calmer calls to action

“Scopri come funziona” sits next to “Inizia”, so users choose between exploring and committing.

R03 · addresses I03

Make matching explicit

Always three proposals, each with a “Why we chose this therapist for you” explanation.

R04 · addresses I04, I05

Decouple preferences from matching

Change schedule or preferences without retaking the full questionnaire.

Iterations

  1. 11 January 2026

    Version 0

    Informal action analysis

    First restructuring: an explanatory entry point, a pill-shaped tab bar instead of the sidebar, a dashboard.

  2. 20 January 2026

    Version 1

    Think-aloud test · SUS 91.67

    Added change and delete appointment, clearer therapy progress, and routes to the therapist profile.

  3. 8 March 2026

    Version 2 · final

    Summative test · SUS 91.25

    Three proposals with match explanations, filters to compare profiles, change therapist from the first screen, quick actions.

07

Final design

83 screens across six flows, built from 29 components and 34 design tokens.

08

Motion

Motion is kept calm and purposeful: content rises in on arrival, confirmations settle with a soft pop, and the live-session indicator pulses. Transitions in the prototype use push for forward navigation, sheets for filters and smart animate for questionnaire progress.

Prototype

10

Design system

Behind the screens is a small design system: 34 variables (10 primitives, 13 semantic colours, 7 spacing and 4 radius values), 14 text styles and 29 components. Every text colour passes WCAG AA contrast and every touch target is at least 44 px.

11

Results

Four participants completed every task on the final version, working alone while recording their screen, with no errors. Scores also became more consistent as the design matured (variance fell from 58.33 to 29.69). Participants singled out the “why this therapist” explanation as what made the matching feel trustworthy.

12

Reflection

The biggest change was structural, not visual: separating “understand the service” from “start the test” fixed three critical issues at once. For a mental-health service, trust comes from transparency: showing how matching works, what it costs and what happens to your data.

The final version adds what a real product would need next: crisis support and health-data consent in the questionnaire, empty and error states, and the online session itself. The samples were small (three to four people per round), so the next step would be a larger test across all age segments.
