Unobravo Redesign

Unobravo connects people with therapists over video call. Its website tried to be a marketing page, a guide and an onboarding flow at once, and the app offered no explanation of the service at all. I redesigned both, from interviews to a tested Figma prototype.

Category
Design
Techniques
UX Design, HCI, Interaction Design, Figma

Notes

The research combined interviews across three age groups, a competitor study of Serenis, expert reviews and think-aloud testing. It surfaced nine usability issues: an overwhelming "how it works" page, calls to action that pushed people into the questionnaire before they understood the service, opaque therapist matching, scheduling that ignored stated preferences, a full test to repeat just to change therapist, no way to compare profiles, hidden pricing and privacy information, and an app whose navigation was buried in a sidebar.

Over three iterations the design added a calmer home page, a real dashboard with the next appointment and pricing, three therapist proposals with a "why you match" explanation, filters to compare profiles, and a way to change therapist by only updating preferences. In the summative test of the final version, all four participants completed every task with no errors, and the average SUS score rose from 75 on the original product to 91.25.
