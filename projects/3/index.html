wipEU

wipEU is an open data project on how the representation of women in positions of power and decision-making correlates across the European Union, and how it has changed over time.

Brief
Data on women in EU decision-making is scattered across EIGE tables, and headcounts hide how far each body is from parity.
Role
Idea, data retrieval, visualisation, quality and legal analysis, website; the 2026 rework on my own.
Method
EIGE datasets for six areas merged into mashups; shares computed from headcounts and checked against EIGE's own percentages.
Outcome
No area reaches parity. The site shows every body as seats, with a pace-to-parity projection and country profiles.
Stack
D3.js · JavaScript · Python · EIGE open data
Date
2024 · reworked 2026
Context
Open Access and Digital Ethics, 2023/24, MA Digital Humanities and Digital Knowledge, University of Bologna
With

wipEU began as a group project with Salvatore Di Marzo and Matteo Guenci for Open Access and Digital Ethics (2023/24). I reworked it independently in 2026; the version shown here is that rework.

Notes

We combined EIGE datasets across six areas (public administration, business and finance, politics, environment and climate change, education and research, law and institutions) into new mashup datasets, and assessed their quality, legal and ethical aspects as open data. In 2023, for example, women made up 20% of the highest decision-making bodies of European financial institutions and led 26.8% of major political parties. In 2026 I redesigned the site around seats: a hemicycle for each body with one dot per real person and a year playback, a pace-to-parity projection, a country map and profiles you can compare and share, and a light/dark switch, set in Newsreader and Public Sans with rose for women and navy for men.

Final project for Open Access and Digital Ethics (2023/24, University of Bologna) with Salvatore Di Marzo and Matteo Guenci. I worked on the idea, data retrieval, the visualisations, the quality and legal analyses and the website, built with Leaflet and Plotly.js. The mashup datasets are released under CC BY 4.0.
